Macros

A macro-construction turns a construction into a tool: from initial objects, it builds final objects.

Creating a macro

  • New macro, in the Macros family of the tools menu or in the Macros menu, opens the definition; the objects selected become its first initials.
  • Step 1: tap the initials, in the order they will be asked for. Any object can be one, even a constructed one. What they determine lights up in green.
  • Next: the finals, then tap the finals among the green objects. With no final chosen, the visible green objects are the finals. An object not determined is refused: what it misses blinks in red.
  • A point on an object whose support is determined is determined too. As a final, it keeps moving on its support after each application; as an intermediate, its place is fixed.
  • The panel gives the macro’s name, the order of the initials (drag their rank), the message shown for each, the help and the icon: drawn from the figure, one or two letters, or an image.
  • Create the macro saves it in the figure. Under an existing name, with other initials, it becomes a new method of that macro.

Applying a macro

  • The figure’s macros follow New macro in the menu; on an object’s ring, the Macros place gives those that take it as their first initial.
  • The hint gives the help and the initial expected (“1/3 – …”). Tap the initials; a click in empty space or on a line creates the point expected; a number is typed in the hint.
  • The result shows as a preview before the last initial. The macro stays in hand: Done or Esc leaves it.
  • The finals are objects like any other (name, style), which don’t move, except a point on an object, along its support.

With the keyboard

  • C, t = IsoscelesTriangle(A, B) applies the macro and names its finals in order; _ leaves one out: _, t = IsoscelesTriangle(A, B).
  • a = RadicalAxis(c, d) for a macro with one final; Parallelogram(A, B, C) alone names the finals after the macro.
  • The initials must come before the application in the construction.

Open the box

  • With a final selected, the bar’s Open the box action replaces the application by its construction: the intermediates appear, named and styled as in the figure of the definition; the finals stay the same objects.
  • Hovered, it previews what will come out of the box.

Library

  • The Macros menu of the menu bar (outside DocTools) opens the Library that comes with DocGeo, in folders: triangles, quadrilaterals, circles, transformations, conics, projective geometry, constructions.
  • Chosen, a macro is copied into the figure, with those it uses, then applies as a macro of the figure. Its name can also be typed: a = RadicalAxis(c, d).
  • If the figure already has another version of the macro, an alert offers to replace it.

My macros

  • Add to my macros keeps a macro of the figure for every figure: it appears in My macros.
  • Manage my macros… renames them, puts them in folders (Conics / Elements) and deletes them.
  • They are kept in this browser only: Export my macros… saves them in a .dgo file, which Import macros… brings into another browser. Importing also reads a figure’s macros; a macro of the same name, defined otherwise, is replaced after confirmation.
