Commands

  • Names ignore case and accents; the English name, in small print, is accepted in every language.
  • A, B, O, P: points; d: line, segment or ray; c: circle or curve; p: polygon or plane; s: segment or solid; u: vector.
  • r, a, k, f: number, expression, slider or formula (Circle(O, 2*r)); n, t: written numbers.
  • The icon is that of the tool in the palette; 3D: forms in space.

Points

Command Form Result
Point Point(c, t) point on the object c (line, circle, curve, locus…) at position t
Point(p, a, b) point on a polygon or a conic. a and b are the coordinates of a point in a frame of the object: its first vertex and the next two (polygon), its center and the ends of its semi-axes (ellipse, hyperbola) or its vertex (parabola). That point is then brought into the polygon or onto the conic. The gesture writes a and b.
Point(s, x, y, z) point on a sphere, a cylinder or a cone, its position given by three numbers
Point(formula) point whose coordinates the formula gives: Point(2*B - A)
(x, y) A = (x, y) free point; (x, y, z) in 3D
M = (x(B) + 1, y(A)) point with at least one coordinate given by a formula
FixedPoint FixedPoint(x, y) fixed point, which cannot be moved; FixedPoint(x, y, z) in 3D
Midpoint Midpoint(A, B) midpoint of [AB]
Midpoint(s) midpoint of the segment s
Center Center(c) center of the circle c
Intersection Intersection(d1, d2) common point of two lines
Intersection(a, b, n) n-th common point (n from 1 to 4) of objects at least one of which is not a line: circles, conics, curves
Intersection(d, p) 3D common point of a line and a plane
Intersection(d, s, n) 3D n-th point (n = 1 or 2) where the line d pierces the sphere, the cylinder or the cone s
Intersection(p, q) 3D common line of two planes
Intersection(s, t) 3D common circle of two spheres
OtherIntersection OtherIntersection(a, b, P) the common point of a and b farthest from P
Incenter Incenter(A, B, C) center of the circle inscribed in the triangle ABC
Centroid Centroid(A, B, C) centroid of the triangle ABC
Circumcenter Circumcenter(A, B, C) center of the circle circumscribed about the triangle ABC
Orthocenter Orthocenter(A, B, C) orthocenter of the triangle ABC
Vertex Vertex(p, k) k-th vertex (from 1) of a polygon or a polyhedron

Transformations

What defines the transformation first, then P, a point or an object: segment, line, polygon, circle, conic, curve…; in 3D also a plane, a sphere, a polyhedron (except Inversion). The image of an object is an object of the same kind, otherwise a locus (see Transforming an object): t2 = Reflect(d, t). GeoGebra’s order (P first) is not accepted.

Command Form Result
Reflect Reflect(O, P) reflection of P in the point O
Reflect(d, P) reflection of P in the line d
Rotation Rotation(O, P, a) image of P by the rotation with center O and angle a (in degrees or radians, depending on the figure)
Translation Translation(u, P) image of P by the translation by the vector u
Dilation Dilation(O, P, k) image of P by the dilation with center O and ratio k
Inversion Inversion(c, P) image of P by the inversion in the circle c

Command Form Result

Formula functions

d, x, sqrt, sin… compute without creating an object: see Formulas and programs.

Polygon sides

Side · Click near a side to use segment tools; click inside to select the polygon.

Within an argument, Side(p, k) refers to side k, counted from 1 in vertex order, without adding a segment to the construction. This also works for regular polygons. 

M = Midpoint(Side(p, 2))
