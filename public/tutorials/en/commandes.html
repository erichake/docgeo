Commands

  • Names ignore case and accents; the English name, in small print, is accepted in every language.
  • A, B, O, P: points; d: line, segment or ray; c: circle or curve; p: polygon or plane; s: segment or solid; u: vector.
  • L: list of points (see Lists of points); a point can also be written in the argument: (2, 3), Point(A + [1, 0]), L(2).
  • r, a, k, f: number, expression, slider or formula (Circle(O, 2*r)); n, t: written numbers.
  • Where a number is expected, a segment stands for its length and an angle for its value, as in GeoGebra: Circle(O, s), Rotation(O, P, α).
  • The icon is that of the tool in the palette; 3D: forms in space.

Transformations

What defines the transformation first, then P, a point or an object: segment, line, polygon, circle, conic, curve…; in 3D also a plane, a sphere, a polyhedron (except Inversion). The image of an object is an object of the same kind, otherwise a locus (see Transforming an object): t2 = Reflect(d, t). GeoGebra’s order (P first) is not accepted.

Reflect Reflect(O, P) reflection of P in the point O
Reflect(d, P) reflection of P in the line d

Rotation(P, a) image of P by the rotation about the origin through the angle a
Rotation(d, P, a) in 3D, image of P by the rotation about the line d through the angle a
Rotation(A, d, P, a) in 3D, image of P by the rotation about the line through A parallel to d, through the angle a

Polygon sides

Side · Click near a side to use segment tools; click inside to select the polygon.

Within an argument, Side(p, k) refers to side k, counted from 1 in vertex order, without adding a segment to the construction. This also works for regular polygons. 

M = Midpoint(Side(p, 2))

On a line of its own, c = Side(p, k) adds a segment: from vertex k − 1 to vertex k, or to the first vertex for the last side, like the sides of a regular polygon whose number of sides is a slider in GeoGebra.

Polyhedron faces

Face · A tool that expects a plane or a polygon, or that places a point, takes the face seen under the pointer; without a tool, a click selects the polyhedron.

Within an argument, Face(k, n) refers to face n, counted from 1, without adding an object to the construction. The base of a prism or a pyramid is face 1. A face behaves like a polygon: you can put a point on it, and it serves as a plane for the tools of space. A line only meets it inside the face. In 3D, any polygon serves as a plane in the same way. 

d = Perpendicular(O, Face(k, 1))
