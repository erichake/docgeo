Magnets, pin and spring

Magnets

A magnetized free point sticks to the chosen objects when it is dragged near them, without being bound to them.

  1. Select the free point, then Magnets. The rest of the figure goes under a veil.
  2. Touch each attracting object (point, line, circle, conic, curve, list): it turns red, with an initial attraction of 20 px. The point and its dependents are excluded.
  3. Set the attraction of the chosen magnet with the slider of its card: None (removes the magnet), 1 to 1,000 px, Everywhere. The attraction zone of each magnet is drawn in pale red.
  4. Finish with a touch on an empty spot, Done or Esc.

The point can be dragged during the setting to try it out. Each setting is an undo step.

Pin

Pin to the screen (free point, plane figure): the point keeps its place in the window, not its coordinates, when the view is zoomed or moved. It can still be moved. Pressing again: removal.

Spring

Animate (point on a line, segment, ray, circle, arc, polygon, conic or locus) brings out under the point a spring ending in a ball.

  • Pulling the ball and releasing it launches the point; while pulling, a ghost of the point already shows the motion.
  • Length: speed, in displayed notches (px/s on a line, a polygon or a conic; °/s on a circle; % on a locus).
  • Side: direction; the point goes opposite to the pull.
  • Across a line (more than 45°): back and forth. Otherwise, the point loops.
  • Below the first notch: stop.

On a point already animated, the spring comes out stretched to the current speed. Pressing again puts the spring away. Exact speed, reverse direction, stop at the end: Animation section of the properties, which also animates sliders.
